# Turret factions: the player's own set

The seventeen turrets are Mindustry's, sprite for sprite
(`public/mindustry/README.md`: placeholder art, GPL, to be replaced before a
commercial release). The swarm has already stopped being Mindustry's: it is
six animals now (`docs/unit-art.md`). This page is the same decision for
the other side of the fight, written as four candidate directions drawn to
the same rules, so the choice is made by looking at them at the size they
draw at rather than by arguing about adjectives.

The concept sprites are in `docs/turret-concepts/`, generated by
`node scripts/turret-concepts.mjs` with a copy of the `game/pixelArt.ts`
engine. They are concepts: four heads and four base plates a direction,
one per footprint, drawn to see whether the direction holds at 32 px and
at 128. Nothing here is wired into the game.

## 1. What a turret set has to be, read off the docs

These are the constraints the art already lives under. A direction that
breaks one is not a direction.

- **A turret is two sprites.** A base plate that never turns, one per
  footprint (`UV_TOWER_BASE1..4`, `towerBaseIcon`), and a head that the
  renderer spins to face its target (`top()` in `game/atlas.ts`, `t.angle`
  in `renderer.ts`). The head is drawn facing up and fills its cell: 32 px
  for a 1x1, 64 for a 2x2, 96 for a 3x3, 128 for a 4x4 (32 px a tile, the
  stock sizes). A set is therefore 17 heads + 4 plates + a palette, and a
  head has to have a FRONT, or the rotation says nothing.
- **The house rules apply unchanged.** Flat plates, four or five butted
  colours, no baked outline (the atlas adds the block rim in `#404049`),
  nothing thinner than two pixels, symmetric by construction, plating that
  runs down the body and never across (a horizontal cut on a symmetric
  shape is a face, `pixelArt.ts` rule 6).
- **No band colour in the art** (`pixelArt.ts` rule 4). The card frame
  says Common / Uncommon / Rare / Ultra in `#C6C6CE / #5A9BF2 / #FFD37F /
  #B07BFF`. A turret whose body is amber or violet lies about its rarity,
  so Mindustry's own `Pal.accent` (`#ffd37f`) is off the table as a body
  colour. Small glows are fine; a dominant hue is not.
- **The swarm owns six accent hues** (`PAL.mech` red, venom acid green,
  star-gold, bomber magenta, harpoon teal, wraith violet) and every body
  wears crux red on its cell. A conquered turret (`Conquest`) is the same
  sprite multiplied by crux red, and a hurt one greys in thirds
  (`HP_TINT`): a set needs enough LIGHT area in every head for the tint
  to read, so an all-dark set fails on the board even if it looks good on
  a sheet.
- **A set is a skin over one roster, never a second roster.** The sim,
  the deal, the mods and the balance tables are keyed by `TowerKind`;
  factions change what a `duo` looks like, not what it does. That is what
  makes "player-side factions later" cheap: `UV_TURRETS` and
  `TOWER_ICONS` become per-faction lookups and nothing under them moves.
- **It ships the way the animals shipped.** Generate at load with the
  pixel engine, pack OVER the stock cells behind a flag (`packAnimalArt`,
  `ANIMAL_ART`), and the stock art stays untouched until the flag is
  removed. A direction is trialled in the built game, zoomed out, with a
  full wave on it, never on a preview sheet.

## 2. The role grammar, shared by every faction

If a second faction ever ships, a player who learned one has to read the
other on sight. So the silhouette cue for a ROLE is fixed across factions,
and the faction supplies the material. This is the same idea as "one
animal per family, one gimmick per family" from the other side.

| role (stock kind) | the cue every faction keeps | Foundry | Verdance | Hive | Reliquary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| twin gun (duo, salvo) | two parallel barrels, a seam between | bolted twin barrels | two seed pods | two stings | two carved slots |
| charge beam (lancer, meltdown) | one wide emitter, a bright slot at the front, capacitors on the flanks | wedge with copper capacitors | sunflower disc with a lens | firefly lantern | jade lens in a gilt frame |
| artillery (hail, ripple) | tube mouths seen from above, on a turntable | four ringed mouths | a hooded pitcher | a mound with vents | an urn's mouth |
| flak / spray (scatter, cyclone) | a flared bell or a fan of short tubes | | | | |
| flame / short reach (scorch, fuse) | a squat body, a wide nozzle | | | | |
| chain / field (arc, parallax, wave, tsunami) | a dish or coil, no barrel at all | | | | |
| the heavy (spectre, foreshadow) | barrels longer than the body | long twin barrels, radiator rails | the flytrap, stalk behind | comb block, four stings | the sun disc |

The four that ship as concepts are the four rows with names in them; the
blank rows are the same exercise, not a different one.

## 3. The four directions

### Foundry — the high-tech set, redrawn as ours

Gunmetal ramp straight off `pixelArt.ts` (`#2c2d38 / #4d4e58 / #7b7b7b /
#c1c3d4`), one copper accent (`#e07a3f`), a warm-white glow. Octagonal
plates, corner bolts, seams that run down the middle of every head.

- *For:* it is the drop-in replacement for the GPL art. A machine reads
  as a STRUCTURE instantly next to an animal swarm, which is the one
  legibility problem every organic direction has to solve. The ammo,
  muzzle and hit effects (`game/weapons.ts`) already look right on it.
- *Against:* it is the least surprising answer, and it keeps the game
  looking like a Mindustry mod even after the sprites are original.
- *Verdict:* the right SECOND faction. It is the "keep the high tech"
  option made original, and factions later need one of these anyway.


### Foundry, five ways

The high-tech direction holds five materials without changing a role
cue. Each is a complete faction on its own; the concept sprites are
`docs/turret-concepts/{foundry,boiler,blackline,ceramic,scrap}-*.png`.

| variant | plate language | palette | note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mill (`foundry`) | octagonal bolted plates, a copper seam | gunmetal ramp, copper `#e07a3f` | the safe fallback; the most Mindustry-adjacent |
| Boiler | cylinders from above, rivet runs, a gauge, a steam stack | iron `#3a3a42/#6a6a74`, brass `#c8973a`, ivory | round against a hard-edged swarm: the strongest silhouette. Brass stays on fittings |
| Blackline | chevron wedges, one cyan light line down the spine | three near-blacks, a pale core plate, strip `#9fe8ff` | the pale core plate is not optional: it is what the hurt and conquest tints read through |
| Ceramic | rounded pale plates, capsule barrels, dark slots | shade/plate/white, signal orange `#ff6a2a` | tints best of the five; needs a darker ramp on ice and salt floors |
| Scrapyard | welded patches, a weld seam, hazard paint on tips and rims | rust `#8a4a2a`, iron, weathered plate, hazard `#e6c34a` | the run is paid for in scrap and the turrets look like what they cost; hazard yellow stays on stripes |

*Verdict:* Boiler or Scrapyard. Boiler for the silhouette, Scrapyard for
the fiction.


## 3a. The first faction: Foundry, the roster

The decision taken: Foundry in Mill's plating is faction one. What a
faction adds on top of the four-head concepts is the rule for the other
thirteen, and it is two rules:

- **One shape a role.** No head is another head at a different size.
  The duo is two barrels; the hail is one fat mortar mouth on a
  turntable; the scorch a flat flared nozzle with a tank behind; the arc
  a coil and a prong with no barrel; the salvo three barrels in a row
  over a magazine; the scatter a bell that flares forward; the lancer a
  wedge with capacitors; the wave a tank with a water window and one
  nozzle; the parallax a dish on a yoke; the swarmer a box of missile
  cells; the fuse a broadside of three wide short tubes; the ripple four
  ringed mouths; the tsunami the great tank with twin nozzles; the
  cyclone a rotary cluster on a drum; the spectre long twin barrels with
  radiator rails; the meltdown one lens and three capacitor banks; the
  foreshadow a single rail with accelerator rings.
- **One accent per ammo, seven in all**, and the accent is the colour of
  the thing thrown, never of the rarity: copper `#e07a3f` for a bullet
  (duo, salvo, spectre), olive `#a8b04a` for a shell (hail, ripple),
  ember `#ec7458` for flame (scorch, fuse), ice `#a9d8ff` for a beam
  (arc, lancer, meltdown, foreshadow), water `#5c6dbb` (wave, tsunami),
  mint `#8fe0b8` for a field (parallax), salmon `#f595be` for a missile
  or flak (scatter, swarmer, cyclone). A player learns seven colours and
  reads a formation's job across the map.

The seventeen are `docs/turret-concepts/mill-<kind>.png`, on Mill's four
base plates; the accent table is in `roster.json` beside them. The
proposed names (Pinion, Lobber, Torch, Sparker, Triplet, Bellow, Kiln,
Sluice, Halo, Quiver, Broadside, Bombard, Floodgate, Grindstone,
Crucible, Furnace, Railspike) are captions only; the stock kind stays the
key everywhere in the code.

### Verdance — plants; the head turns toward the swarm like a heliotrope

Bark (`#5a3d26`), leaf ramp (`#2f6b3a / #5aa84a / #a6de7c`), blossom coral
(`#ff8a6b`) and a pale cream seed. Every head is a plant seen from above:
a whorl of leaves, a pod, a flower disc, a pitcher, a flytrap.

- *For:* the fiction writes itself — the swarm is animals, the ground
  defends itself, and a flower head that TURNS to track a target is the
  rotation the renderer already does, read as behaviour. It is the
  direction nobody else's tower defence has, and a coral blossom against
  darksand is the best-contrasting hero colour of the four.
- *Against:* organic vs organic. A plant on a grass floor (`grass1.png`)
  against a green frog family is the worst case, and rot (the venom
  line's poison) landing on a plant reads as the plant's own colour.
  Both are palette fixes: the leaf greens are deep and blue-shifted
  against venom acid, and the base plate is bark so the footprint stays
  dark on any floor.
- *Verdict:* **the flagship.** Ship this one first.

### Hive — chitin and wax; the insect look the swarm was refused

Chitin (`#3b2a22`), shell (`#8f5a2b / #c98a4a`), wax (`#f1e3b6`), a honey
accent kept small (`#f2b632` sits near the Rare amber band), and a pale
blue-white sting. Wasp abdomens, a firefly lantern, a termite mound, comb.

- *For:* `docs/unit-art.md` threw the insect silhouette out of the SWARM
  because the legged rig made every animal a spider. A turret does not
  walk, so the look is free, and it is unclaimed on the board. Fixtures
  (mound, comb) are naturally static and heavy, which is what a turret
  wants to be.
- *Against:* insects vs animals is a fight on the same page of the field
  guide; at default zoom the two sides could read as one ecosystem. The
  honey accent has to stay small or it argues with the Rare frame.
- *Verdict:* the third faction, and the one to hand a "swarm-flavoured"
  player build to.

### Reliquary — old stone, jade and gilt; the guardians of a ruin

Stone ramp (`#5d564c / #9a8f7c / #d3c7ad`), jade (`#3d9a72`), gilt
(`#e3b752`), one ember for a lit mouth. A carved ward, a lens in a frame,
a censer, a sun disc.

- *For:* it contrasts with the swarm on every axis at once — still
  against moving, mineral against animal, warm stone against crux red —
  and it answers "why are animals attacking a base" with a picture: a
  ruin the wild is reclaiming. The maps are already rock and sand.
- *Against:* it is the hardest to make FIRE convincingly. Bullets and
  beams out of a censer need their own effects, and the sun disc has no
  barrel, so "where is it pointing" leans entirely on the one bright ray.
  Gilt is a near neighbour of the Rare band and has to be kept off large
  areas.
- *Verdict:* the wildcard. Worth one more round if the plant set lands
  and the effects pass is cheap.

## 4. What a faction IS, mechanically, when it comes

- A **faction** is a name, a palette, 17 head drawings, 4 base plates,
  and a recolour of the shared effects (muzzle, trail, hit) keyed by
  role. Nothing in `TOWERS`, `TURRET_RARITY`, `mods.ts` or `relics.ts`
  knows it exists.
- `UV_TURRETS` (renderer), `TOWER_ICONS` and `towerBaseIcon`
  (`towerIcons.ts`) take a faction; the icon pipeline (`turretIcon`) is
  unchanged because the drawings go through the same outline pass.
- The save carries one field, the faction, chosen on the deploy screen
  next to the difficulty — regular runs pick it, custom runs may roll it,
  the same split `GameMode` already makes.
- A base plate is the faction's ground, so a formation of one faction
  reads as one building from a distance; that is the second thing the
  plates are for after "where the head sits".

## 5. How to trial one

1. Draw the 4x4 first, facing up, symmetric, filling a 128 grid. If it
   does not read as its role (long barrels, a disc, tube mouths) in
   silhouette, stop.
2. Draw the 1x1. If the material still reads at 32 px with the rim on,
   the direction works; fill in the other fifteen.
3. Pack the heads and plates over the stock cells behind a flag, exactly
   as `packAnimalArt` does, and look at a Nemesis wave 30 on darksand and
   on grass, zoomed out. The questions are only two: does a turret read
   as a structure next to the swarm, and does a hurt or conquered one
   still read as hurt or conquered.
