Animechs · codex art

Ten mutator faces, stamped flat

All ten rules redrawn as 32×32 pixel art on Mindustry's own palette — three to six colours each, no outline, no rim light. Shown at the sizes the game renders them, inside the band-coloured frame each screen already draws.

32 is not an arbitrary pick. Mindustry's status effect sprites — its icons for exactly this thing, a rule laid over a body — are 32×32, and its block art is 32px to the tile. These faces sit beside that art, so they are drawn on its grid.

What changed, and what it costs

Flat plates, not a lit edge

The first pass shaded every face by tracing light down its top-left edge and shadow down its bottom-right. That makes an icon look embossed — pressed out of the page like a button. Mindustry's blocks look stamped flat: go and count the colours in the vendored lancer.png or mender.png (upstream keeps its own filenames) and you get four or five, butted against each other along straight edges, with no dark contour anywhere.

So the shading model here is one primitive: draw a plain rectangle, clip it to whatever the face has already drawn, and you get a top plate or a skirt that follows the silhouette exactly. Form comes from parts, not from a light source.

Three faces fought back

Armored Swarms took three tries. Stacked horizontal bands made a cloud sitting on a funnel; a plate inside a rim with a seam across it made a helm with a visor and a row of teeth. Anything symmetrical about a vertical axis cannot become a face — so it is a riveted strake now, which is also how cleaver and repeater are built.

Hungry Mechs was a duck: a deep pale top plate read as a head and a glinting eye finished the bird. Thin bands, one flat eye, no glint. Speedy's streaks were gunmetal, two shades off the ground, and vanished the moment the face left the codex tile; they are trailing ember now.

The band moves to the frame

A face used to be a single tinted path, because the tint was how a player read a rule's weight. Two of the three screens don't need it to be: the codex tile draws a 3px band-coloured border and the deploy chip a 1px one, so the weight is answered by the frame either way.

The third is the progress track, where a mutator chip wears the codex's pink ring and the face was the only thing carrying the band. That signal is gone. It is a fair trade — the chip beside it holds a full-colour turret sprite, so a full-colour mutator face is more at home on that row, not less — and the comment in Progress.tsx that claimed the weight was still readable there has been corrected rather than left to rot.

Shipped. The faces are drawn by components/mutationArt.ts and rendered by MutationFace — art as code, the way tiles.ts already draws the floors, so there is no sprite sheet and no checked-in SVG to fall out of step with it.
